## The lessons in brief

1. **Every sentence says something specific, in standard words.** Lloyd's central comment on the
   draft was that the writing was "oblique ... couched in wordy language that does not impart
   information."
2. **No coined terms.** He replaced "digestion peptide" ("a new term that you made up") with
   "proteolytic peptide", and rejected "observability-aware" outright.
3. **No hype, and no vague lists.** "Leveraging" is "a hype word". "Such as" is "vague and
   non-specific" when you know every item, so name them.
4. **Rebuild the argument; don't polish the words.** A careful word-level pass on the Introduction
   was rejected as too timid. The A+ abstract was rebuilt on the shape of the 2021 ProteaseGuru
   abstract, which Lloyd had accepted: the field and why it matters, the tool, "We present here", one
   sentence per feature followed by what it lets the reader do, then named examples.
5. **End the background on a gap the reader can check**, resolve it paragraph by paragraph, and close
   the Conclusions on the same argument.
6. **In a methods or software paper, follow the path the reader takes through the software**, one
   capability per paragraph. The workflow section must be the plainest in the paper.
7. **Every edit is also a fact-check** against the code, the figures and the cited papers. On this
   paper that caught a false claim about mass limits, a figure that contradicted its text, the wrong
   license, and features the GUI doesn't offer. Those features were deleted, not hedged.
8. **Check the PI's suggested wording too, and say when you depart from it.** He suggested "nucleic
   acids", but the tool has no DNases, so the paper says "RNA".
9. **Unmarked text is not approved text.** Apply his comments to the parts he didn't reach, and treat
   a highlight with no comment as a flag.
10. **If his Word draft is the live text, never regenerate it from markdown.** Edit with tracked
    changes under the user's name. Keep every comment alive through Accept All by putting each
    replacement inside the comment's range. Test Accept All and Reject All in real Word. An accepted
    section shows no markup at all, not even comment shading.
