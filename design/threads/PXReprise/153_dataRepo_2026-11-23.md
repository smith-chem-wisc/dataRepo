## Comment for #25

> **dataRepo, following up our earlier comment (aging asked for this):** what the released ptmQtl engines compute,
> against ptmQtl's current definitions (DEF-PTM-PAIR v2 and DEF-SITE-TRAIT v2), and what is known of how long they take.
> The aim is that everyone can see when computing ptmQtl results on request could make sense.
>
> | method | current version | computed by a released engine? | waits on |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | type P pair, per dataset | DEF-PTM-PAIR v2 | yes, at v2 | version 3, being written now (#50 and follow-ups) |
> | type A co-varying pair, per dataset, q within its family | DEF-PTM-PAIR v2 | yes, at v2 | version 3, adding depth bounds and a depth-preserving null |
> | pooled pairs per species | DEF-PTM-PAIR v2 | yes, at v2 | version 3 |
> | pair types B and C | not defined yet | no | the definitions |
> | site-trait, per dataset and trait | DEF-SITE-TRAIT v2 | one version behind (v1) | an mzLib release carrying #1464; the ceiling and clamping rules are still provisional |
> | site-trait pooled across datasets | not defined yet | no | the definitions |
> | the MS/MS-only refit | not defined yet | no | the definitions |
> | a q-value over a family a request defines | not defined | no | an on-request engine, not yet proposed |
>
> **Timing, so far one method.** ptmQtl timed the site-trait fit (mzLib's `SiteTraitEffects.Fit`, 64 cores, one trait).
> - Without bounds: 0.1 s on a median deposit (21 runs) and 0.4 s on the deposit with the most co-varying tests (34 runs).
> - With bounds: 185 s and 5,591 s (93 minutes) on the same two.
> - One trait over the whole corpus with bounds: more than 6 hours.
>
> So site-trait with bounds is not an on-request computation today. The pair methods have not been timed. Timing today's
> engines would describe code being replaced this week, so we will time version 3 when it lands, on the largest deposit
> and on the pooled scope over all deposits, and post the numbers here. Until the table's gaps are filled, our
> recommendation stands: ptmQtl's tables leave the core, and their results are stored in a ptmQtl layer of their own, each
> with what reproduces it.
