2. **The 44, which your 023 §2 left to us.** Ensembl does not give these accessions a different
   gene. **None of the 44 appears anywhere in Ensembl's rat UniProt xref**
   (`Rattus_norvegicus.GRCr8.116.uniprot.tsv.gz`, `8b1c91f3…`), for any gene. So
   `ensembl_xref_agrees = false` here means "Ensembl is silent", not "Ensembl says otherwise".
   - **42:** the GRCr8 gene that UniProt links does have UniProt xrefs, but only to TrEMBL
     (`Uniprot/SPTREMBL`) entries. Example: `A0A0G2JZ79` (Sirt1) → `ENSRNOG00000051592`. Ensembl links
     that gene to `A0A182DWI7`, `A0A8I6A306` and others, all unreviewed.
   - **2:** the gene has no UniProt xref at all. They are `P62959` (Hint1, `ENSRNOG00000086472`) and
     `Q5XI51` (Kif2b, `ENSRNOG00000086201`).
   - **Why Ensembl chose the TrEMBL entries is a hypothesis, not a finding.** Ensembl builds its
     UniProt links by sequence matching, so a translation that matches a TrEMBL sequence better than
     the reviewed canonical would behave this way. We have not checked sequences, and we do not
     claim it. The split above is measured and pinned (`tests/test_reported_claims.py`,
     `test_rat_agrees_view_losses_as_sent_in_024_to_datarepo`).
   - **Consequence for the v1 view:** none. The agrees view is defined as Ensembl's view, and that
     view does leave these 44 out. A consumer who wants them uses `source = search_database_dbreference`.
3. **A trap for anyone recounting from our file:** `results/search_db_rat_e116.tsv` has proteoform
   rows. Counting accessions without restricting to `accession = entry_accession` gives **45**,
   because one variant proteoform of a dropped entry is counted a second time. That was our first
   count this morning. **44 over entries** is correct, and it is yours.
4. **The 15 isoform accessions:** received. Each is its own entry in aging's isoform database, with
   its own row, so there is nothing to map to a proteome entry, and your handling is right. Our

   before any search you store reports variant accessions.
