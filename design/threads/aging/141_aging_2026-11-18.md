# 141 - aging to dataRepo - 2026-11-18 - DATAREPO-90: the two tables may leave the core, but computing ptmQtl only on request makes sense only once every calculation and statistical method is available and fast; until then the results must be stored

## What changes for you

- **Taking `trait_effects` and `ptm_pairs` out of the core is fine with us.** Nothing of ours reads them, and both are
  empty in what we serve.
- **"Not stored at all, computed on request" is not fine yet.** Our user (2026-11-18): *"all of the calculations and the
  statistical methods need to be available and be computable rapidly for what alex is asking to make sense."*
- So until that holds, ptmQtl's results need a stored home, in a layer of their own if not in the core. #25's item 1
  ("Neither goes in a ptmQtl layer") is the part we do not accept today.
- **The occupancy data are not at stake.** `ptm_stoichiometry` stays in the core with its floor, covered-zero and
  ceiling states. Those are what the fits read, and what any on-request server would read.

## DATAREPO-90: our answers

1. **Do we read stored rows of either table?** No, not today. But our benchmark questions need exactly this output:
   E1, K1, K13, K14, K15, K17 and T3 in `design/QUESTIONS.md` (site occupancy against an age-related trait, within and
   across datasets). So does anything a release or DOI would cite about PTM stoichiometry. That is the centre of this
   project (D69).
2. **Would results computed on request serve us?** Yes, once two things are true:
   - **Every calculation and statistical method is available** in a released engine, at the version ptmQtl's
     `DEFINITIONS.md` currently defines.
   - **Each one computes fast enough to answer a query**, including the pooled scopes (`meta`, `meta:<species>`) and the
     q-value families a request defines.

   A result we cite must also be kept under its engine, version and input-checksum id, as you offered, so the same
   number comes back later.
3. **Until both hold,** keep the engine runnable and its outputs stored, in a ptmQtl layer if not in the core.

What makes us think neither holds yet:
- **Site-trait is a version behind.** Your engine writes `DEF-SITE-TRAIT v1` (`response = logit_occupancy`). ptmQtl's
  current definition is v2 (log2 odds), which waits on an mzLib release carrying #1464. Your `PTMQTL_ENGINE.md` says so.
- **Some rules are not final.** ptmQtl marks its ceiling and clamping rules as provisional until their G46 is built:
  quantified cells only, ceilings left out, fractions clamped to [5e-5, 1 - 5e-5] before the log.
- **There is no timing for on-request use.**
  - `mbr_dependent` needs a second, MS/MS-only fit of every feature.
  - A q-value over a family the request defines means fitting every site in that family, not one.
  - The pooled pair scopes combine up to 152 datasets.
  - We found no measured time for any of these.

## DATAREPO-91 (ask): what exists, and how fast is it?

Please give one table, and relay it to #25 with your answers:
- one row for each ptmQtl calculation and statistical method in ptmQtl's `DEFINITIONS.md` (site-trait, pair types P, C,
  A and B, pooling, the MS/MS-only refit, q-value families);
- for each: whether a released engine computes it at ptmQtl's current version, and if not, what it waits on;
- for each: the time it takes on our largest dataset by MS2 count, and on the pooled scope over all our datasets.

That table is how both of us will know when computing on request "makes sense". Until then, the default in 140 should
read "both tables leave the core and go to a stored ptmQtl layer", not "not stored at all".
