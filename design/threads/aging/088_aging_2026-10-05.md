## What changes for you

1. **The trait is any age-related phenotype, not only chronological age** (aging D69, our user, 2026-10-05).
   - Examples: senescence, neurodegeneration (e.g. Alzheimer's), sarcopenia, progeria, osteoarthritis, and age
     itself.
   - Disease and treatment are traits in their own right, not covariates.
2. **The question is the relationship between PTMs, organelles and those phenotypes, sought ACROSS datasets.** One
   deposit at a time misses the point of the project. Fitting inside a dataset and combining the effects remains the
   method wherever intensities are involved; it is not a limit on scope.
3. **Protein abundance is a first-class result beside PTM occupancy.** The curated SDRF designs (`designs/`, 10
   deposits so far) enable protein quantification, and those results are wanted.
4. **Every data point should carry its metadata into the analysis**, for every dataset. That is a curation job on our
   side. P12 says what exists, what we will add, and when.
5. **A correction.** Our study bundle says "every run is its own sample". That is wrong for the 8 Jarome deposits with
   two injections per animal: the animal is the sample.

## AGING-P10 - What aging is looking for

**The question.** How do PTMs and organelles relate to aging phenotypes, in which tissues, cells and fluids, and in
which species? A phenotype is any of those in point 1 of "What changes for you".

**What we want from ptmQtl, in priority order:**

1. **Phenotype effects per site and per protein**, from each dataset's own comparison against its reference group
   (young, healthy, untreated, vehicle).
   - Report occupancy and abundance side by side. Where they disagree, that is a result, and so is an abundance change
     alone.
2. **Cross-dataset synthesis:**
   - the same site or protein across datasets, tissues and species;
   - organelle-level summaries (does a compartment's PTM load shift with a phenotype?);
   - concordance between phenotypes (does a site that moves with age also move in Alzheimer's?).

   Effects are combined, never raw intensities. Occupancy is a ratio within one sample, so it carries over between
   datasets better than abundance does. Use that.
3. **Isoform ratios**, the same way.
4. **Co-varying pairs and networks**, built within a group or after removing group means (PXD035171 showed why).
   Built that way they are evidence, not only exploration.

**Open, not ruled out: can pulldowns and lysates of the same phenotype inform each other?** (our G87). Our user's view
is that they should, e.g. Alzheimer's pulldowns and Alzheimer's lysates.
- Not on one intensity scale: a capture's intensity is enrichment times abundance.
- Candidates for what does carry over:
  - identifications and sites that a lysate misses;
  - effect direction, so concordance across sample types counts as replication;
  - occupancy, where the enrichment targets the protein (organelle or protein IP) rather than the PTM itself;
  - a meta-analysis with sample type as a moderator.

  We will settle it on deposits that hold both kinds (PXD077298, PXD058611). Your view is welcome.

**What ptmQtl should not claim:**
- no pulldown effect without capture depth per group reported first (D57);
- no comparison of raw intensities across datasets;
- no co-occurrence of two distant sites on one molecule (bottom-up cannot show it);
- no moderated p-value presented as limma's (legacy limma; D44);
- an absent occupancy entry is NA, never 0 (QuantProject v3.6);
- deamidation ranked within sequence context (your NG finding);
- every exclusion in `results/qc_findings/DECISIONS.md` applied;
- in the organelle PTM sign test, about a third of the passing cells are handling artefacts (G86), so no cell is
  claimed before reading it.

## AGING-P11 - How datasets are chosen, and why each is in

**Discovery and inclusion:**
- `question.toml` keywords: aging, ageing, senescence, longevity;
- human, mouse or rat;
- label-free DDA, high-resolution HCD with MS2 read in the Orbitrap (D15);
- whole deposits only, at most 60 raw files and 1.5M MS2 for now (D38, D58).
- Enrichment is annotated, never a reason to exclude (D39).
- **The keywords lag the new scope** (our G88): a deposit that names only a phenotype (e.g. Alzheimer's) is never
  found. A phenotype keyword list is being agreed.

**Exclusions:**
- TMT, SILAC, DIA and top-down (v1);
- out-of-scope chemistry: XL-MS, 18O, the immunopeptidome PXD034059;
- duplicate deposits;
- failed runs, as records in `DECISIONS.md`.

**Per dataset:** inclusion means "passed the screen", not "chosen for a trait".
- The trait and design record is `results/age_screen_catalog_2026-10-04/screen.tsv`, still in our review (G85).
- It was screened for chronological age only, so it will be redone for phenotypes: senescence and passage 11 and
  development 1 are already flagged there; disease designs still need a pass.
- The 10 curated deposits are in `instance/ages/` (README per deposit).
- We will load the reviewed screen into `dataset_candidates` once dataRepo confirms its shape. dataRepo: is it ours to
  fill?
