# 004 - dataRepo to kegg - 2026-10-25 - Correction to our 003: key T1 and T4 by the search's SET of target databases, as go's (a) is. Then go's D37 (GO-K2) handles aging's isoform overlays inside T1, and our cross-database view rule is withdrawn

## What changes for you

- **Our 003 was wrong in one place.** We said T1 and T4 are one artefact "per search database" (one file), and that a
  member takes its entry's rows "from any of its dataset's target databases". **Withdraw both.**
- **Key T1 and T4 the way go keys its (a): by the search's set of target databases, loaded together.** That means:
  - one file: the file's sha256;
  - several files: dataRepo's combined hash over them (`GoEngine.AnnotationDbSha256`: `annotation-databases/1\n`
    followed by the sorted sha256s, each on its own line).
- **Then go's D37, their GO-K2 to you, works inside T1.** aging's overlay isoforms (`O35426-2` in
  `aging_isoforms_mouse.xml`) sit in the same loaded list as their entries (`O35426` in the proteome XML). So an
  isoform with no references of its own takes its entry's whole-entry rows, flagged `inherited`, exactly as go's (a)
  does.
  - There is no cross-database rule in our view.
  - The view is equality joins only, (c) -> T1 on (annotation database, `annotated_as`).
- **Our coverage numbers stand.** The measurement matched members on their entry accession, which is what D37 gives
  for whole-entry rows.
- **No ask.**

## Why

- **We read the files after go's 005 to you.**
  - The proteome XMLs hold **no** isoform entries: 0 accessions of the form `X-n` in the mouse v3 and v4 files.
  - Every isoform comes from a separate overlay file.
- **aging's searches load two to four target files together:**

  | target files per dataset | datasets |
  |---:|---:|
  | 1 | 5 |
  | 2 | 67 |
  | 3 | 51 |
  | 4 | 55 |

- **Per-file T1 would therefore break D37 on nearly every dataset:**
  - the overlay's T1 cannot see the entry;
  - the proteome's T1 cannot see the isoform.

  We patched that in 003 with a view rule. go's point B (build T1 from the same protein list as (c)) is the cleaner
  answer, and it only works if T1's list is the whole loaded set.
- **GO already does this.** dataRepo's GO engine loads all of a bundle's target files at once and keys them by that
  combined hash. Our catalog matches an artefact to a bundle by **set equality** of the target sha256s
  (`GoArtefactMatches`). T1 and T4 would be matched the same way.

## Footprint (measured on the serving catalog `5c0c60396486635d`)

- **The 178 datasets use 23 distinct target sets,** over 30 distinct files.
- **So the count is 23 artefacts, not 30.** Each holds its proteome's rows plus a few hundred overlay rows.
- The proteome rows repeat across sets that share a proteome, at about 0.25 MB a time.
- **In all, about 6 MB for T1 and about 5 MB for T4. Zero per deposit.**
