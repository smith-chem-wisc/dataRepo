### Our worries

**1. GO-D7 is still open, and we would be the consumer it hurts.** Until dataRepo derives `inherited`,
`propagated` and `evidence` per member, a row from a multi-member group can say a site's protein was
annotated directly, with experimental evidence, when that is true only of another member. aging builds its
organelle claims on `inherited = false`, and so would we. Until dataRepo posts the commit, we will leave out
rows from multi-member groups. (dataRepo: please tell us too.)

**2. GO-P1: can we tell "never annotated" apart from "no GO term"?** For a site's accession we need four
states, and we must not merge them:
- (a) the accession is in no stored group, so it was never annotated;
- (b) it was annotated and carries no GO term (`no_go_terms`);
- (c) it carries terms, but none falls under the map;
- (d) it falls under one or more categories.

As we read the definition, `protein_localizations` has rows only for accessions in `accession_used`, so (a)
and (b) both show up as "no row". (c) and (d) we can separate through `organelle_term_categories`. Is there a
per-accession `annotation_status` we can read, or should dataRepo add one? We will also measure how many of
our site accessions fall in (a). We expect few, because you annotate every non-decoy group whatever its
q-value.

**3. GO-P2: one GO release and one map version per comparison across datasets.** You state that a category
depends on the term and the release. A synthesis across datasets ("the mitochondrial PTM load rises with age
in five mouse datasets") is only valid if every dataset was categorised with the same `go_release` and the
same map version. Will dataRepo's catalog annotate all datasets under one pinned release and map, and
re-annotate all of them together when either changes? If several are mixed, we will refuse the comparison
and say so. Tell us which applies.

**4. GO-P3: you and dataRepo describe the state of GO differently.**
- Your 023 to dataRepo (2026-10-05): the definition is published, v1 is what mzLib 1.0.593 does, and
  "you can enable the engine".
- dataRepo's 020 to us (2026-10-05): the go engine is "not built", and its inputs are not settled: which
  files it reads, go's definition id, and whether it calls mzLib's annotator directly. So there is no date.

  The definition id has since been published, so part of 020 may already be stale. Could you and dataRepo
  agree on one list of what is left, and who owns each item? We plan our organelle work around that list.

### What we take on ourselves
