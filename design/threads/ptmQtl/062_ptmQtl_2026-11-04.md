## Why the shape works for us

- **The lab rule is ours, and your measurements show it cannot rest on one field.**
  - Lab-head contact ids are a fresh copy per deposit, and no lab head has an ORCID.
  - A submitter account can be a core facility serving several labs.
  - The named lab head can be the mass-spectrometry collaborator (PXD006815).
  - Names vary: "PhD" as a last name, and Kron spelled three ways.
- **So we will combine evidence:** the lab head's name, normalized by us; shared papers (DOI); and shared raw files.
  That needs the raw fields untouched, which is what your shape keeps. We write the rule when we build the site prior
  (G42, D65), and we will send it to you before it is used.
- **E-mail:** the rule does not need it, and leaving personal contact data out is right.

## Why a served view, not the query alone

- **Pooling treats deposits as independent.** Two things depend on it:
  - pooled co-varying pairs combine deposits by Stouffer's Z;
  - the site prior's rule (D65) is that the same raw data deposited twice counts once.
- **PXD033430 and PXD057544 share 3 of their 9 runs.** Counted as two deposits, those three runs count twice.
- **Pooled results are rebuilt at every catalog publish, by your build (D61).** The duplicate check has to run every
  time, without anyone remembering to run a query. A view built at publish does that, and every consumer sees the
  same answer.
- **A duplicate within one deposit matters too.** PXD006815's two runs that are one file would read as two animals
  in perfect agreement in any per-deposit fit (site-trait, co-varying pairs).
- **What would serve us** (your call on names and the exact columns): one row per run whose `sha256` appears in more
  than one run, with `sha256`, `dataset_id`, `file_name`, and whether the other copies are in another accession or
  the same one. That joins straight to `runs`.
