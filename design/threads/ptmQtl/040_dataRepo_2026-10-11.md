## DATAREPO-P30: `sequence_window`

- **Column:** `sequence_window` on `ptm_sites`, as you suggest. It is written by the ingest step that already checks
  every site's residue against the searched sequence.
- **The rule, as we read your 038:**
  - 15 characters: the site's residue and 7 on each side, `_` past either end;
  - an isoform takes its own sequence;
  - a terminal site takes the window of the residue it sits on, the residue `ptm_sites` already keys it on. That is
    residue 1 for a protein N-terminus, as you say, and the last residue for a protein C-terminus. Correct us if the
    C-terminus should differ.
- **When:** in 1.4.0.
  - It is a new core column, so every stored bundle needs a re-ingest to carry it.
  - 1.4.0 already needs that re-ingest (mzLib #1403's ProForma change), and it is the release that merges your
    engine. So the windows and the engine that compares them arrive together.
- **Sites with no sequence: none today.**
  - Every site in aging's 113 stored bundles found its residue in the searched sequence: 1,885,950 `residue_matches`
    and 0 `no_sequence`, `wrong_residue` or `beyond_length` (`bundle.json` `protein_databases.site_residue_check`).
  - A site that ever has no sequence gets a NULL window and does not pool, as you say.
- **Reference case v3:** the column is confirmed, so you can build it.

## DATAREPO-P31 (ask): one accession, two different sequences

The ingest reads every database a search used, and one accession can appear in more than one: a UniProt proteome
plus aging's isoform and PTM files. If two of them give one accession different sequences around a site, which
window? *Our default:* none (NULL), counted in `bundle.json` beside `site_residue_check`, so the site does not pool.

**Measured on aging's 113 stored bundles and the 28 databases they searched:**
- **411 of 1,885,950 sites** would get two different windows, in 25 bundles.
- **All 411 are on two keratins, O76011 and P19013.** MetaMorpheus's contaminant panel carries older sequence
  versions of both: O76011 at 436 residues (version 2, 2009) against the proteome's 394, and P19013 at 534
  (version 4, 2005) against 520. No accession repeats with a different sequence within one file.
- **This also shows a defect of ours.** Our ingest places a site against every sequence an accession has, so on these
  two proteins one modification is written twice, at positions 42 (O76011) or 14 (P19013) apart, with the same PSM
  count. In PXD022180, for example, `Carbamidomethyl on C` sits at O76011 351 and 393. We will fix it at 1.4.0's
  re-ingest. Under the default above, none of these sites pools either way.
